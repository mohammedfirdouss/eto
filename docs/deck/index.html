Ẹ̀tọ́ 01 / The Problem
01
The_Problem

The reform everyone
believes in is
not law.

Since July 2025, headlines have promised Lagos renters monthly rent, capped agency fees, and jail for illegal eviction. The bill behind those headlines is still in committee.

Tenants who trust the headlines refuse to pay for protections that have not passed, and lose. Tenants who miss the real law accept illegal evictions it already forbids.

Even the law's name is unstable: careful sources call the same statute both the "2011 Law" and "Cap. T1, 2015". If that trips lawyers, an ordinary tenant has no chance.

Days since second reading
435
The bill passed second reading on 10 July 2025. It is still not law. That number is the product's front page.
Verified_2026-09-17
The Fact
Lagos has no rent control. Increases of 50% to 200% happen at renewal. What tenants believe about the law decides what they accept.

The_Users

A locked door.
A short notice.
A fee with no law
behind it.

A yearly tenant in Surulere gets one month to leave. The law entitles her to six. She does not know, so she packs.

A landlord changes the locks over arrears. That is a crime today, arrears or not. Nobody in the compound knows.

An agent quotes a fee "as the government directed". No such law exists.

Built for renters first; also for small landlords who want to stay compliant, and the paralegals who handle these disputes daily.

Notice she was entitled to
6 mo.
Section 13(1)(e), in force since 2011. The most common protections are the least known.

Ẹ̀tọ́ 03 / The Solution
03
The_Solution

Two panels,
never merged.

Every answer splits into LAW · IN FORCE and BILL · NOT LAW YET. Each panel is stamped with the date its status was last verified.

Every sentence carries a section chip. Tap it and the verbatim statute opens, with a link to the official gazette or Assembly page.

A user must be unable to mistake a proposal for a protection. That single design rule is the product.

Sections & clauses, all citable

A track-changes ledger shows what the bill would actually change, row by row, cited on both sides. Including the uncomfortable rows:

Ẹ̀tọ́ 08 / Scalability
08
Jurisdiction_Packs

The engine knows
nothing about
Lagos.

Everything local lives in one directory: statute, bill, bodies, myths, ledger, languages, manifest. Retrieval, date arithmetic, citations, and documents are domain-general.

Scaling means authoring a pack, not rewriting code. The pattern travels: nearly every Nigerian state, and Nairobi, Accra, and Kampala, has an old law in force, a loudly reported reform, and a gap between them.

The second pack is already started the honest way: Abuja, sources identified, labelled unverified until the checklist is done.

AI_In_The_Build

AI built it.
A human kept it
honest.

Claude Code did the engineering: corpus pipeline, retrieval, rules engine, UI, 41 tests, and live browser verification of every phase. Firecrawl ran the ingestion jobs that traced the "10%" myth and caught the conflicting official contact pages.

The human supplied the idea and the corrections that mattered: the statute's real name, the four exempted areas, a disclaimer aimed at the wrong reader, a design that copied too closely.

Runtime AI in the product
None
Deliberately. A legal tool must never hallucinate a section number. Retrieval is deterministic, dates are code, letters are templates.
